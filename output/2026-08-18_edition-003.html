De-risking by design: public guarantees, AI offtake and a US$750m iron-air raise mark out where capital feels safe

Edition #003 18 August 2026 Since Monday’s edition ≈ 5 min read

Every deal in the past day is an exercise in manufacturing safety for capital. In Romania, a solar-plus-storage hybrid stacks development banks, an InvestEU guarantee and a 15-year CfD under a single financing. In the US, growth equity hands iron-air developer Form Energy US$750 million while a multi-day storage startup converts AI data-centre demand into a 1 GW deployment agreement. Ørsted signs up the operator for its Hornsea 3 battery a year before it runs. And a pointed new analysis argues Europe’s banks will lend to hydrogen only where demand is captive — everything else, for now, stays equity’s problem.

Solar PV

Romania’s biggest CfD award shows the full de-risking stack in one deal

Econergy has signed financing agreements for its Părău 2 project in Brașov County — 342 MW of solar PV plus a 150 MW battery — with a six-lender consortium spanning the EBRD, the Black Sea Trade and Development Bank, Exim Banca Românească, NLB, OTP and Intesa Sanpaolo Romania, for a package of roughly €229 million. Around US$132 million of the main facility sits under an InvestEU guarantee, and the project carries the largest capacity award in Romania’s CfD mechanism to date: 125 MW at US$57.18/MWh, fixed for 15 years from commercial operation. Financial close is expected within about three months.

The anatomy is the story: multilateral debt, a public guarantee and a long-dated CfD layered under a merchant-flavoured hybrid — the complete de-risking toolkit applied in a market that has, within two years, become one of Europe’s busiest for storage-backed solar.

Storage / BESS

Long-duration storage gets its two missing ingredients: growth capital and a demand anchor

Iron-air developer Form Energy has secured a US$750 million Series G round led by T. Rowe Price — among the largest single equity cheques the long-duration segment has seen. The demand side moved on the same day: multi-day storage startup Noon Energy signed a 1 GW deployment agreement with Sabanci Renewables for US AI infrastructure, turning data-centre load into exactly the kind of captive offtake that lenders have been demanding from newer chemistries.

Construction news reinforces the platform pattern: Fluence and utility LEAG have started building their second large-scale German BESS under the partnership that is also delivering Europe’s largest battery project, while in Australia Ausgrid’s PLUS Grid Storage broke ground on the 200 MW/400 MWh Steel River East system — its first project delivery.

Wind

Ørsted contracts the revenue layer a year early; Türkiye opens a new pre-FID pipeline

Ørsted has selected Arenko’s platform to operate the battery storage at Hornsea 3, due to start operations next year — a wind major locking in the optimisation layer of a co-located asset well before commissioning, the same operations-first logic that ran through yesterday’s Coalburn close. On the pipeline side, Türkiye will hold its first offshore wind tender in early 2027; the auction design will decide from day one whether the new market produces bankable revenue streams or stranded seabed. And in the US, Revolution Wind is down to its final four turbines — construction risk quietly retiring on a project whose sector faces loud permitting noise elsewhere.

Hydrogen

Where the lending boundary actually sits

A sharp piece of analysis published today argues that Europe’s banks are treating hydrogen as a niche, not an economy: credit flows where demand is captive and credible — refineries, fertiliser, industrial clusters — while merchant hydrogen still fails on offtake, margins and debt capacity. The engineering keeps moving regardless: the Port of Antwerp-Bruges now hosts a commercial-scale AEM electrolyser from Power to Hydrogen, producing high-purity hydrogen for real industrial users. The gap between what can be built and what can be financed remains the sector’s defining spread — and the number to watch stays the same: the 2.5-year financial-close clock that starts for the EU auction winners in Q4.

Capital & Policy

Distributed flexibility gets a policy tailwind on two continents

California’s Assembly Appropriations Committee advanced two bills to expand virtual power plants and cut energy rates, while New South Wales introduced upfront discounts of up to 40% for commercial and apartment battery installations. Neither moves a project-finance model on its own — but both widen the aggregatable resource base that VPP and tolling revenues are increasingly built on.
